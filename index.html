Mapping Interprovincial Trade Barriers in Canada

Canada's provinces, territories and federal government each write their own regulations. Where those rules diverge, a product that is legal to sell in one province can be unlawful in the next. Some of these divergences exist for good reason, such as differences in climate, culture or circumstances. Some, however, impede the integration of the Canadian economy without yielding wider benefits. This project does not tell the difference between the two, but aims to place the debate on Canada's internal and external trade integration on a firmer evidence basis. To this end it has compiled and analyzed the full text of every regulation in the country to find where barriers may exist.

Why it mattersContributing to three debates

From better regulation, to fewer internal barriers and international trade diversification, this project provides a new evidence base to inform public policy debates.

01

Cutting red tape?

A lighter regulatory load can create a more vibrant Canadian business environment. But not every requirement is an unnecessary hindrance and cutting the wrong regulation may expose the public to risk. Knowing what requirements exist and how they differ from jurisdiction to jurisdiction can bring useful metrics to this debate.

02

Internal trade barriers persist because standards diverge

Goods lawful in one province can be unlawful in another because the two governments point to different technical standards, or to different editions of the same standard. This is a barrier to internal trade that is rarely visible from the text of either rule. The project makes it visible, and specific enough to be acted on.

03

Standards alignment shapes how far Canada can diversify its trade

Whether Canadian rules line up with United States standards, or diverge province by province, affects the ability to trade both within Canada and with partners beyond the US. Mapping where Canadian law adopts foreign standards, and where it does not, is part of understanding the room there is to diversify.

The research projectReading the whole regulatory corpus, not only samples

Most work on regulatory burden or interprovincial barriers rests on surveys or on a hand-picked set of rules. This project starts from the full, consolidated regulations of all ten provinces, three territories and the federal government, and treats them as one body of text that can be measured consistently. Working from the complete corpus is what makes a fair comparison between jurisdictions possible, and what lets a divergence be located to the exact provision where it sits.

The measures build on published methods. Restriction counts follow the RegData approach from the Mercatus Center; the four semantic dimensions come from Alschner, Zachar & Pontello (2018); the standards layer is a rule-based extraction over a register of 43 standards bodies, with the most-cited standards audited by hand.

Where the project is going

Now · Phase 1
The corpus and the first measuresBuilding the full-text corpus, and the two explorers on this site: what each jurisdiction's regulation looks like by the numbers, and which technical standards it incorporates.
Next · Phase 2
Finding the divergencesComparing rules across jurisdictions to detect where they genuinely differ, and grouping them by industry and by the goods or issues they govern — the level at which a specific barrier to internal trade becomes visible.
Later · Phase 3
From divergence to barrierSeparating the differences that obstruct trade from those that reflect a genuine local choice, and setting out where alignment would help.

Using this workWho it is for, and how to cite it

The site is meant for the Canadian public, policymakers, researchers and journalists. The two explorers are written to be read without a background in either law or data: each opens with headline findings and ends with a plain statement of what the numbers can and cannot show.

  • Each explorer carries its own limits — most importantly, standards reached only through an adopted building or fire code are not yet visible, and restriction counts are a provisional version that runs slightly high. Levels are provisional; rankings are stable.
  • Every figure is traceable. All numbers come from release 2026_1.1, frozen 15 October 2026, so any figure here can be placed against an exact state of the law.
  • Data and code will be public. The corpus build, the analysis layer and these pages are being prepared for release in an open GitHub repository so the work can be reproduced and extended.
Research in progress

This is Phase 1 of a continuing study. The findings here are stable enough to explore and to report, but the project is ongoing — read each explorer's method and limits before citing, and check back as the later phases add the divergence, industry and product views.
