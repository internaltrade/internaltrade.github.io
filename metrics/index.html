Industry view is not built yet

Grouping regulations by NAICS industry needs a mapping from each regulation to the sectors it governs. The corpus carries each regulation's enabling statute (3,516 distinct parent acts), which is the natural bridge to NAICS, but the industry coding itself is Phase 2 work and isn't in release 2026_1.1 yet. When that mapping lands, this tab will show the same metrics per industry.

◇

Product / issue view is not built yet

This view would group regulations by the goods or issues they regulate — the level at which an interprovincial divergence like differing construction-site toilet-seat requirements becomes visible. It depends on a product/issue classification of each provision, which is active Phase 2 research and isn't in the corpus yet.

Standard cut.
Body only. Schedules (fee tables, prescribed forms) are excluded from every measure — provinces break them out at very different rates.
English only. Restriction counting is calibrated on English; French text is excluded rather than scored near-zero. Quebec's figures are its English-language regulations.
RegData v1. Restriction counts run about 12% high versus a pending v2 (word-boundary, no cross-measure overlap). Treat levels as provisional; rankings are stable.
Pooled. A province's value is its total hits over its total words, so a few thousand short municipal orders don't swamp it. Age is the median years since last amendment, shown only where dates exist.
Sources — check the methodology. RegData restriction counting (shall, must, may not, required, prohibited) and the published federal counts: QuantGov RegData Canada (Mercatus Center). The four semantic dimensions and their wordlists: Alschner, Zachar & Pontello (2018). Applying RegData's exact five-term lexicon to this corpus gives 90,944 federal restrictions, within a few percent of RegData Canada's published ~86,000–90,000.
